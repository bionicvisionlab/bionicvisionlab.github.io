Lily M. Turkstra

PhD Candidate·PBS

she/her·3201C BioEngineering

lturkstra@ucsb.edu

Lily Turkstra is a PhD candidate in Psychological & Brain Sciences at UC Santa Barbara. She studies the human side of sight restoration, asking what happens after vision is restored and how people learn to make useful sense of artificial vision.

Her research examines how people who are blind use assistive technologies, how visual prosthesis users experience artificial vision in daily life, and how attention, engagement, and cognitive state shape performance. Her dissertation focuses on visual rehabilitation, including gamified training and neurofeedback, with the long-term goal of developing personalized approaches that help people make better use of restored vision.

Lily earned her B.S. in Psychology, with minors in Biology and Music, from Cal Poly San Luis Obispo, where she conducted psychoacoustic research in the Multisensory Perception Lab. Before beginning her PhD, she served as lab manager of the Bionic Vision Lab.

Outside of the lab, Lily enjoys tidepooling, playing bass, and biking around Santa Barbara.
